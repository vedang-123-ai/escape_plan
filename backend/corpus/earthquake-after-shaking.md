# Earthquake Safety — After the Shaking Stops

The moment the ground stops shaking it is important to take action quickly and safely
[cite: shakeout-guidance-after].

## First minutes

- Check for injuries and damage that need immediate attention, and use first-aid training to assist those
  in need [cite: shakeout-guidance-after].
- Look around for new hazards — leaking gas lines, damage to the building, water or electric lines — that
  may be dangerous, especially if there are aftershocks [cite: shakeout-guidance-after].
- **Be prepared for aftershocks and stay away from anything that looks like it may fall**
  [cite: shakeout-guidance-after].
- Protect yourself with sturdy shoes and work gloves against broken glass and debris, plus a dust mask and
  eye protection [cite: shakeout-guidance-after].

## Gas: the rule is conditional, and the reverse is absolute

**Shut off the main gas valve only if you suspect a leak** — because of broken pipes, the odor or sound of
leaking natural gas, or a meter spinning quickly [cite: shakeout-guidance-after]. Only the gas company can
turn the gas back on after they check for leaks, so shut it off only if necessary
[cite: shakeout-guidance-after].

The USGS states the other half plainly: **do not turn the gas on again if you turned it off — let the gas
company do it** [cite: usgs-earthquake-donts]. Do not use matches, lighters, camp stoves, barbecues,
electrical equipment or appliances until you are sure there are no gas leaks
[cite: usgs-earthquake-donts]. ShakeOut gives the same instruction for the immediate aftermath: do not use
candles or matches, because you could start a fire and there may also be gas leaks — use flashlights,
battery-powered lights and light sticks [cite: shakeout-guidance-after].

This asymmetry matters for an app: shutting the gas off is a **judgement call with a named trigger**, while
turning it back on is **never the resident's action**.

## Other utilities and damage

- **Electrical.** Shut off power at the main breaker switch if there is any damage to house wiring, and
  leave it off until the damage is repaired [cite: shakeout-guidance-after].
- **Masonry.** Stay away from chimneys and walls made of brick or block; they may be weakened and could
  topple during aftershocks. Do not use a fireplace with a damaged chimney — it could start a fire or let
  poisonous gases into the home [cite: shakeout-guidance-after].
- **Fire.** If you are trained and have an extinguisher handy, put out small fires immediately and call for
  help without waiting for the fire department. **Large fires are a sign to evacuate**
  [cite: shakeout-guidance-after].
- **Spills.** Clean up spilled medicines and non-toxic substances with caution; isolate or cover harmful
  materials such as bleach, lye, garden chemicals and petroleum products with an absorbent. When in doubt,
  leave the home [cite: shakeout-guidance-after].
- **Telephone.** Do not use the telephone except for a medical or fire emergency
  [cite: usgs-earthquake-donts].

## When to evacuate

Outside a tsunami zone, **evacuate your home only if there is damage to the building or the surrounding
area is unsafe** [cite: shakeout-guidance-after]. If the building is undamaged it can be safe to remain even
if the power is out, though an alternative location is worth finding if power is not restored within a few
days [cite: shakeout-guidance-after]. If you do go to a shelter, take only a grab-and-go bag with
essentials such as medication, important documents and prescription eyewear — shelters have limited space
[cite: shakeout-guidance-after].

This is the opposite default from fire. After an earthquake, **staying in an undamaged building is the
sourced recommendation** [cite: shakeout-guidance-after]; in a fire, leaving immediately is.

## Expectations

The USGS is blunt that help may not be immediate: **do not expect firefighters, police or paramedics to
help you** [cite: usgs-earthquake-donts]. This is the grounding for the app's position that a household
plan is a self-reliance measure for the first period after a major quake, not a substitute for emergency
services.
