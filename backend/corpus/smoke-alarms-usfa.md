# Smoke alarms: placement and maintenance (USFA/FEMA guidance)

Paraphrased from USFA's smoke alarm guidance [cite: usfa-smoke-alarms]. This document drives the app's smoke-alarm checklist items and the assistant's answers to placement questions.

## Where to put smoke alarms

- **Inside every bedroom / sleeping room.** [cite: usfa-smoke-alarms]
- **Outside each separate sleeping area** (e.g. the hallway serving the bedrooms). [cite: usfa-smoke-alarms]
- **On every level of the home, including the basement.** [cite: usfa-smoke-alarms]
- Mount alarms high — on the ceiling or high on a wall — because smoke rises. Keep alarms away from the kitchen stove (roughly 10 ft / 3 m) to reduce nuisance alarms. [cite: usfa-smoke-alarms]

## Maintenance

- **Test every alarm monthly** using the test button. [cite: usfa-smoke-alarms]
- Replace 9-volt batteries at least once a year; replace them immediately when an alarm chirps a low-battery warning. [cite: usfa-smoke-alarms]
- **Replace every smoke alarm 10 years from its manufacture date.** [cite: usfa-smoke-alarms]
- Interconnected alarms (when one sounds, all sound) provide the earliest warning, especially in multi-level homes. [cite: usfa-smoke-alarms]

## Why it matters

- Almost **three out of five home fire deaths** occur in fires in properties with no smoke alarms (41%) or with smoke alarms that failed to operate (16%). [cite: nfpa-smoke-alarms-report]
- The death rate per 1,000 reported home fires is **about 55% lower** in homes with working smoke alarms than in homes with none or non-working alarms. [cite: nfpa-smoke-alarms-report]

## Accessibility variants

For household members who are deaf or hard of hearing, alarms with strobe lights and bed-shaker/vibrating accessories are available and recommended (see `special-considerations.md`). [cite: usfa-smoke-alarms]
