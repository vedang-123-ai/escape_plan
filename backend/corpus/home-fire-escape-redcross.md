# The Red Cross home fire escape plan and two-minute drill

Paraphrased from American Red Cross home fire preparedness guidance [cite: redcross-escape-plan] and the Red Cross "two minutes to escape" campaign [cite: redcross-two-minutes]. This document is the primary grounding for the app's drill/practice mode (S4.2).

## The two-minute reality

In a home fire, you may have **as little as two minutes to escape safely** from the time the smoke alarm sounds. Fire spreads fast; planning and practicing an escape ahead of time is what makes a two-minute escape achievable. [cite: redcross-two-minutes]

## Building the plan (Red Cross steps)

1. **Find two ways out of every room** — usually the door, plus a window or second doorway — in case the first path is blocked by smoke or flames. [cite: redcross-escape-plan]
2. **Check that exits are usable**: paths clear of clutter and furniture, windows open easily, screens removable. [cite: redcross-escape-plan]
3. **Pick an outside meeting place** a safe distance from the home where everyone goes after escaping. [cite: redcross-escape-plan]
4. **Walk the plan with everyone in the household**, including children, and assign help for anyone who needs it. [cite: redcross-escape-plan]

## Running a fire drill (the app's drill mode mirrors this)

- Put one adult in charge of sounding the smoke alarm and running the drill. [cite: redcross-escape-plan]
- Sound the alarm, **start a timer**, and have everyone move to the meeting place. [cite: redcross-escape-plan]
- Stop the timer when everyone reaches the meeting place — the goal is **under two minutes**. [cite: redcross-two-minutes]
- Practice at least **twice a year**, varying conditions (night drill, blocked primary exit). [cite: redcross-escape-plan]

## During a real fire

- Get low and go — crawl under smoke. [cite: redcross-escape-plan]
- Get out and stay out; never re-enter for people, pets, or things — tell firefighters instead. [cite: redcross-escape-plan]
- Call 911 once safely outside. [cite: redcross-escape-plan]

## Preparedness fast facts

- Only about **26% of families** have actually developed and practiced a home fire escape plan. [cite: redcross-escape-fact-sheet]
- Home fires are the nation's most frequent disaster. [cite: redcross-two-minutes]

## How the app uses this (editorial note)

Drill mode implements exactly this loop: select a household member → walk their computed primary route waypoint-by-waypoint → end at the meeting point card → mark plan as practiced (timestamped, feeds the preparedness score). The timer target shown to users is the Red Cross two-minute benchmark.
