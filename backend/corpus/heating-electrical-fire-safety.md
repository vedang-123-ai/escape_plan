# Heating, Electrical, and Appliance Fire Safety

This document covers fire hazards related to heating equipment, cooking, electrical systems, and clothes dryers, drawing from NFPA safety guidance [cite: nfpa-heating-safety] [cite: nfpa-cooking-safety] [cite: nfpa-electrical-safety].

## Portable and Space Heater Safety

Portable and space heaters require careful placement and use to prevent fires [cite: nfpa-heating-safety].

- **3-foot clearance:** Keep anything that can burn — furniture, bedding, curtains, clothing, and paper — at least 3 feet (0.9 m) away from heating equipment [cite: nfpa-heating-safety].
- **Turn off when unattended:** Always turn off portable heaters when you leave the room or go to sleep [cite: nfpa-heating-safety].
- **Plug directly into an outlet:** Plug space heaters directly into a wall outlet. Never use an extension cord or a power strip with a space heater — the high current draw can overheat the cord and start a fire [cite: nfpa-heating-safety].

## Heating Equipment as a Fire Cause

According to the NFPA report covering the 2016-2020 reporting period, heating equipment is the second leading cause of home fires in the United States [cite: nfpa-heating-report]. Space heaters (portable and fixed) account for a disproportionate share of those fires and an even larger share of the associated deaths [cite: nfpa-heating-report]. The peak months for home heating fires are December, January, and February [cite: nfpa-heating-report].

## Cooking Fire Safety

According to the NFPA report covering the 2014-2018 reporting period, cooking is the leading cause of home fires and home fire injuries in the United States [cite: nfpa-cooking-report]. Unattended cooking is the primary contributing factor [cite: nfpa-cooking-report].

- **Stay in the kitchen:** When frying, grilling, boiling, or broiling food, stay in the kitchen. If you must leave, even briefly, turn off the stove [cite: nfpa-cooking-safety].
- **Keep combustibles clear:** Keep anything that can catch fire — oven mitts, towels, food packaging, curtains — away from the stovetop and oven [cite: nfpa-cooking-safety].
- **Never leave frying unattended:** Frying with oil is particularly dangerous. If oil starts to smoke, carefully turn off the burner. Never throw water on a grease fire [cite: nfpa-cooking-safety].

## Electrical Safety

Electrical failures and malfunctions are a significant cause of home fires [cite: nfpa-electrical-safety].

- **Extension cords are temporary:** Extension cords are intended for temporary use only. If you regularly need more outlets, have a qualified electrician install additional permanent receptacles [cite: nfpa-electrical-safety].
- **Never under rugs or through doorways:** Do not run extension cords under rugs, carpets, or across doorways. The cord can overheat when covered, and foot traffic can damage the insulation [cite: nfpa-electrical-safety].
- **Overloaded outlets and power strips:** Do not overload outlets or power strips. Plugging too many devices into a single circuit can cause overheating and ignite surrounding materials [cite: nfpa-electrical-safety]. Never daisy-chain power strips (plug one into another) [cite: nfpa-electrical-safety].
- **Major appliances direct to outlets:** Major appliances (heaters, dryers, refrigerators) should always be plugged directly into a wall outlet, never through an extension cord [cite: nfpa-electrical-safety].

## Clothes Dryer Safety

Failure to clean the dryer is a leading factor contributing to clothes dryer fires [cite: usfa-dryer-safety].

- **Clean the lint filter:** Clean the lint filter before and after each cycle [cite: usfa-dryer-safety].
- **Lint as ignition source:** Lint is a highly combustible material that can accumulate both in the dryer and in the dryer vent [cite: usfa-dryer-safety].
- **Inspect the vent duct:** Inspect the venting system behind the dryer to ensure it is not damaged, crushed, or restricted [cite: usfa-dryer-safety].
