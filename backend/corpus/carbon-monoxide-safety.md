# Carbon monoxide alarms: placement and safety (NFPA and USFA guidance)

Carbon monoxide (CO) is a colorless, odorless, and tasteless gas produced by the incomplete combustion of fuels such as wood, gasoline, coal, natural gas, propane, oil, and methane [cite: nfpa-carbon-monoxide]. Breathing high levels of CO can be fatal [cite: usfa-carbon-monoxide].

## Where to install carbon monoxide alarms

- **Outside each sleeping area:** Install a CO alarm in a central location outside each separate sleeping area, in the immediate vicinity of bedrooms [cite: nfpa-carbon-monoxide].
- **On every level of the home:** Place alarms on every level of the home, including basements, and in other locations required by applicable laws or standards [cite: nfpa-carbon-monoxide].
- **Near fuel-burning equipment:** If fuel-burning appliances (such as a furnace, water heater, fireplace, or gas stove) or an attached garage are present, install CO alarms to ensure early warning [cite: usfa-carbon-monoxide].
- **Interconnected alarms:** For the best protection, interconnect all CO alarms throughout the home so that when one sounds, they all sound [cite: nfpa-carbon-monoxide].

## Maintenance and testing

- **Test monthly:** Test CO alarms at least once a month using the test button [cite: nfpa-carbon-monoxide] [cite: usfa-carbon-monoxide].
- **Replacement:** Replace CO alarms according to the manufacturer's instructions, typically every 5 to 7 years [cite: nfpa-carbon-monoxide].
- **Never run generators indoors:** Operate portable generators outdoors only, far away from all windows, doors, and vents [cite: usfa-carbon-monoxide].

## What to do if an alarm sounds

- Immediately move to fresh air outdoors or by an open door or window [cite: nfpa-carbon-monoxide].
- Account for everyone in the home and call 911 from fresh air [cite: nfpa-carbon-monoxide]. Never re-enter until emergency responders clear the home.
