# Tornado Safety and Preparedness

This document provides severe weather and tornado safety guidelines based on information from NOAA/NWS [cite: noaa-tornado], FEMA [cite: fema-tornado], and the American Red Cross [cite: redcross-tornado]. 

## Safe Location and Sheltering

When a tornado warning is issued, you must seek shelter immediately in a designated safe room [cite: redcross-tornado]. 
- The safest location is a FEMA-rated safe room or storm shelter, or a basement [cite: fema-tornado]. A basement or underground shelter provides near-absolute protection against extreme winds and flying debris that can easily destroy above-ground walls [cite: fema-tornado].
- If a basement is not available, move to the lowest floor and find the most interior room—such as a bathroom, closet, or hallway—putting as many walls as possible between you and the outside [cite: noaa-tornado].
- Stay far away from windows, doors, and outside walls [cite: noaa-tornado].

## Mobile Homes and Unsafe Structures

Mobile homes, manufactured homes, trailers, and RVs are never safe during a tornado [cite: redcross-tornado]. Even if a mobile home is tied down, no interior room qualifies as a safe room because the structure itself can be destroyed [cite: noaa-tornado]. 
- If you are in a mobile home, you must leave immediately and seek shelter in a nearby sturdy building [cite: redcross-tornado].

## Watch vs. Warning and Alerts

- **Tornado Watch:** Conditions are favorable for tornadoes. Stay alert and monitor NOAA Weather Radio or local alerts [cite: noaa-tornado].
- **Tornado Warning:** A tornado has been sighted or indicated by radar. Seek shelter immediately [cite: noaa-tornado].
- Ensure you have multiple ways to receive alerts, including a NOAA Weather Radio and phone notifications [cite: redcross-tornado]. A bed-shaker or strobe-light alert system is crucial for the deaf and hard of hearing to receive these warnings [cite: noaa-tornado].

## Protecting Yourself During a Tornado

- Once in your safe room, protect your head and neck with your arms [cite: redcross-tornado]. 
- Seek additional protection by getting under a sturdy piece of furniture, and cover yourself with blankets, pillows, or coats to guard against flying debris [cite: noaa-tornado]. Wearing a helmet (such as a bicycle or motorcycle helmet) is also recommended [cite: noaa-tornado].
