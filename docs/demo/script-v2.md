>
> **Target:** table-read under 3:15.
> **Tone:** talking to a parent about their house. Plain, warm, specific. No "leveraging AI",
> no "revolutionary", no "cutting-edge". Numbers only if they're in `backend/corpus/`.

**Total VO: 236 words ≈ 1:34 at 150 wpm.** (Fits well within 3:15 limit).

---

## Beat 1 — Hook

> **VO:** When a smoke alarm goes off, you may have as little as two minutes to get out.
> Two minutes, in the dark, with everyone half-asleep.
>
> But the Red Cross says only about a quarter of families have made and practiced an escape plan. So we built Escape Plan.

---

## Beat 2 — The honest scan

> **VO:** It starts with your phone. You scan your home, and it builds a 3D model.
>
> The scan gets us most of the way there, but it's never perfect. So we built a full 3D editor so you can fix any mistakes—like adding a missing window or connecting stairs. Because a plan on a wrong map is worse than no plan.

---

## Beat 3 — The money shot & hazards

> **VO:** Then it computes. Every room gets a primary escape route, and a secondary route through a different exit.
>
> The routes are calculated by a deterministic engine we wrote. We even added a fire drill simulation, where you start a fire in any room and watch everyone reroute around it. It also flags object hazards, warning you if furniture narrows a path.

---

## Beat 4 — Validation & prevention

> **VO:** We showed this to a working firefighter. He thought the idea was really cool, and he pointed us toward prevention and education.
>
> So the app builds a readiness score and a checklist for fire, earthquakes, and tornadoes. Our fire module is fully quantified, checking route timing and window sizes. The earthquake and tornado modules use FEMA and NOAA readiness guidance. And if you opt in, you can share a snapshot of your plan with your local fire department.

---

## Beat 5 — Grounded AI

> **VO:** Finally, we included an AI assistant to chat about your home and explain safety tips.
>
> Every safety answer is grounded in FEMA, NFPA, or Red Cross guidance and cites its source. If it can't back something up, it says so and points you to your local fire department. The AI explains, but it never computes a route. Because a safety app can't guess.
